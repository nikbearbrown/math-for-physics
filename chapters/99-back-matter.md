## Acknowledgments

This volume owes its shape to the students who arrive in the calculus-based introductory sequence fluent in high-school physics but unsure of the mathematics underneath it — and who asked the right question: not "what is the formula" but "where does it come from." Every chapter here is an attempt to answer that question on the page. Thanks are due to the colleagues who taught the companion physics chapters whose cold opens and worked examples are the gravitational center each mathematical chapter orbits — the Tacoma Narrows bridge, the leaning ladder, the rocket equation, the plucked string — and to the reviewers who caught errors of fact and emphasis. Any that remain are the author's.

## A Note on the Volume

Volume 1 is the mathematics behind the introductory mechanics-and-waves sequence: dimensional analysis, algebra and proportionality, trigonometry, vectors, functions and graphs, limits and derivatives, integrals, linear systems, differential equations, complex exponentials, series, and the wave equation — each derived for its own sake and motivated by one concrete physics problem. No college calculus is assumed; it is built where the physics needs it. Where a topic exceeds introductory scope — the Fourier transform, eigenvalues and the deeper linear algebra, multivariable and vector calculus, the partial differential equations of fields — the text points forward to **Volume 2** (*Mathematics for Physics, Vol. 2 — Advanced Tools*), the refresher behind the advanced physics curriculum, which picks up exactly where this volume stops. Math is the subject; physics is the motivation.

## A Note on Figures

The figures for this volume — the multi-method comparison of a linear system solved three ways, the geometry of intersecting lines and the vanishing determinant, the overlaid plots of $x(t)$, $v(t)$, $a(t)$ for the oscillator, the three damping regimes, the rotating phasor whose shadow traces a cosine, the Taylor partial sums hugging the sine curve, the traveling and standing waves side by side, the progressive Fourier synthesis of a square wave, and the decibel scale on a logarithmic axis — will be added in a dedicated illustration pass. The advanced mathematical tools that several chapters point toward are developed in **Volume 2**.
