### Historical attributions (web-verified)

1. **Mādhava of Sangamagrāma (c. 1340–1425) and the Kerala school — sine, cosine, arctangent series with error terms, ~250 years before Newton and Taylor; transmitted via Nilakantha's *Tantrasangraha* and the *Yuktibhāṣā*.** CONFIRMED. Mādhava (MacTutor: 1350–1425) discovered the sine, cosine, and arctangent power series; results were recorded in Sanskrit verse in Nilakantha's *Tantrasangraha* (c. 1500) and derived/proved in the *Yuktibhāṣā* (16th c.). The transmitting sources named in the chapter are exactly right. Timing: Mādhava c. 1400 vs. Newton c. 1665 (~265 yr) and Taylor 1715 (~315 yr) — "roughly 250 years before Newton and Taylor" is a fair approximation. The "[contested — see pantry]" priority flag is appropriate.
   URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhava_series ; https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Madhava/

2. **Brook Taylor, *Methodus Incrementorum Directa et Inversa* (1715) — the general Taylor expansion.** CONFIRMED. MacTutor: Taylor published *Methodus Incrementorum* in 1715, containing the theorem now bearing his name.
   URL: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Taylor/

3. **Colin Maclaurin, *A Treatise of Fluxions* (1742) — the a = 0 (Maclaurin) special case.** CONFIRMED. MacTutor: Maclaurin published the 2-volume *Treatise of Fluxions* in 1742; the a = 0 special case is named for him (though he credited Taylor).
   URL: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Maclaurin/

4. **Newton, generalized binomial theorem (c. 1665; *Epistola prior*, 1676) — (1+x)^n for real n.** CONFIRMED. Standard attribution: Newton developed the generalized binomial series c. 1665 and communicated it in the *Epistola prior* to Leibniz (1676).

5. **Lagrange, *Théorie des fonctions analytiques* (1797) — the remainder term making truncation rigorous.** CONFIRMED. Standard attribution for the Lagrange form of the remainder; *Théorie des fonctions analytiques* (1797) is the correct work.
