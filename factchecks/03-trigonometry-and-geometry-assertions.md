### Finding 1 — Āryabhaṭa (499 CE) first sine table; jyā→jiba→jaib→sinus etymology

- **Category — Verdict:** EVIDENCE/SPECIALIST — CONFIRMED
- **Assertion type:** POSITIVE (named-figure attribution + etymology)
- **Sentence:** "the Indian mathematician Āryabhaṭa (499 CE) replaced the chord with the *half-chord*, which is our sine. (The word 'sine' is a chain of translation accidents: Sanskrit *jyā* → Arabic *jiba* → misread as *jaib*, 'fold/bosom' → Latin *sinus*.)"
- **Claim checked:** Āryabhaṭa, 499 CE, first sine (half-chord) table; the jyā → jiba → jaib → sinus translation chain.
- **Site visited:** Wikipedia "Āryabhaṭa's sine table"; arXiv:2309.13577 "Aryabhata and the Construction of the First Trigonometric Table"; Britannica "Trigonometry: India and the Islamic World," via WebSearch.
- **Finding:** Confirmed. The *Āryabhaṭīya* (499 CE) contains the first known sine table (table of sine-differences), using the half-chord (*ardha-jyā*, shortened to *jyā*). The etymology chain is the standard account: Sanskrit *jyā/jīvā* → Arabic transliteration *jiba* (written *jb*, vowels omitted) → later read as *jaib* ("pocket/fold/bosom") → translated by Gherardo of Cremona (12th c.) into Latin *sinus* ("bay/cove/fold"). The chapter's gloss "fold/bosom" for *jaib* and the arrow chain are accurate.
- **Expert review needed:** No.
- **Suggested reference:** MacTutor biography of Āryabhaṭa; Wikipedia "Āryabhaṭa's sine table." Already reflected in Sources.
- **Notes:** Resolves the chapter's standing concern about whether the Āryabhaṭa/etymology claim is "laundered" — it is the genuine, well-documented account.

### Finding 2 — Mādhava (c. 1400) power series for sine and cosine, ~250 years before Newton/Leibniz

- **Category — Verdict:** EVIDENCE/SPECIALIST — CONFIRMED
- **Assertion type:** POSITIVE (named-figure attribution + priority claim)
- **Sentence:** "The Indian mathematician Mādhava (c. 1400) found the full power series for sine and cosine — $\sin\theta = \theta - \theta^3/6 + \cdots$ — roughly 250 years before Newton and Leibniz; the small-angle forms are just its leading terms."
- **Claim checked:** Mādhava (Kerala school, c. 1400) found the sine and cosine power series ~250 years before Newton/Leibniz.
- **Site visited:** Wikipedia "Madhava of Sangamagrama," "Madhava series," "Kerala school of astronomy and mathematics," via WebSearch.
- **Finding:** Confirmed. Mādhava of Sangamagrāma (c. 1340–c. 1425), founder of the Kerala school, is credited with the infinite-series expansions for sine and cosine (the "Newton" power series) and arctangent. Newton rediscovered the sine/cosine series c. 1669; the gap is roughly 250–270 years, matching "roughly 250 years." The series $\sin\theta=\theta-\theta^3/6+\cdots$ is correct, and the small-angle forms are indeed its leading terms.
- **Expert review needed:** No.
- **Suggested reference:** MacTutor biography of Mādhava; Wikipedia "Madhava series." Already reflected in Sources (Yuktibhāṣā, Kerala school).
- **Notes:** The *Yuktibhāṣā* (c. 1530, Jyeṣṭhadeva) records these results; attributing the discovery to Mādhava (c. 1400) is standard. No issue.
