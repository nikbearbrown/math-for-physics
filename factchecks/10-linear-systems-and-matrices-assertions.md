## Full Findings

### Historical attributions (web-verified)

1. **Nine Chapters (*Jiuzhang Suanshu*), Fangcheng chapter, array elimination "two millennia before Gauss" (c. 150 BCE).** CONFIRMED. MacTutor dates the matrix-method text to the Han Dynasty (200 BC–100 BC); the counting-board column-reduction it describes is precisely Gaussian elimination, used by Gauss only in the early 19th century. The book's "c. 150 BCE" sits inside the accepted range.
   URL: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Matrices_and_determinants/

2. **Sylvester coined "matrix" in 1850; Latin for "womb," array "out of which" determinants are born.** CONFIRMED. MacTutor: "The first to use the term 'matrix' was Sylvester in 1850. Sylvester defined a matrix to be an oblong arrangement of terms and saw it as something which led to various determinants from square arrays contained within it." The womb etymology (Latin *matrix* = womb/source) and the "out of which determinants are born" gloss match Sylvester's own conception.
   URL: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Matrices_and_determinants/

3. **Seki Takakazu (1683) and Leibniz (1693) independently introduced determinants as a solvability test.** CONFIRMED. MacTutor: Seki wrote *Method of solving the dissimulated problems* in 1683 introducing determinants; "The first appearance of a determinant in Europe was ten years later. In 1693 Leibniz wrote to de l'Hôpital" giving the zero-determinant solvability condition. (Note: the *word* "determinant" was coined later by Gauss 1801/Cauchy 1812; the chapter correctly speaks of the *concept*, not the term.)
   URL: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Matrices_and_determinants/

4. **Cramer published his rule for general n×n systems in 1750 (*Introduction à l'analyse des lignes courbes algébriques*); Maclaurin had special cases earlier (contested priority).** CONFIRMED. MacTutor: Maclaurin's *Treatise of Algebra* (written 1730s, published posthumously 1748) "contains the first published results on determinants proving Cramer's rule for 2×2 and 3×3 systems"; "Cramer gave the general rule for n×n systems in a paper ... (1750)." Maclaurin knew the rule by 1729; Cramer's correct sign rule is why the name stuck. The chapter's "[contested — see pantry]" flag is appropriate and honest.
   URL: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Matrices_and_determinants/ ; https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Cramer/

5. **Cayley, "A Memoir on the Theory of Matrices" (1858) — matrix algebra as such.** CONFIRMED. MacTutor: "Cayley in 1858 published *Memoir on the theory of matrices* which is remarkable for containing the first abstract definition of a matrix ... defining addition, multiplication, scalar multiplication and inverses." Published in *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society* 148.
   URL: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Matrices_and_determinants/
