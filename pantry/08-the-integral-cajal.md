# CAJAL — Ch.08 The Integral

**Fig 08-01 — Riemann rectangles converging to the area.**
- Concept: the definite integral is the limit of a sum of thin rectangles f(x_k)Δx hugging the curve as Δx → 0.
- Type: two-panel curve-with-rectangles (coarse vs fine partition).
- Shows: a velocity curve v(t) over [0,T] with a coarse staircase of rectangles (visible gaps) beside a fine staircase (gaps nearly gone), the sliver v(t_k)Δt labeled, sum → ∫v dt.
- SCOPE include: the curve, both partitions, one highlighted sliver, the limit statement. EXCLUDE: FTC, antiderivative arithmetic.

**Fig 08-02 — The Fundamental Theorem links slope and area.**
- Concept: the accumulation function A(x)=∫f has derivative f — area-finding and slope-finding are inverse operations.
- Type: paired panels — area under f, and the accumulation curve A whose slope is f.
- Shows: left, f(x) with the swept area A(x) shaded to a moving edge x and the thin growth strip f(x)dx; right, A(x) rising with its slope at x equal to f(x); the boxed ∫ₐᵇ f = F(b)−F(a).
- SCOPE include: f with growth strip, A with slope read, dA/dx=f(x), FTC2 box. EXCLUDE: Riemann convergence (its own figure), impulse/spring examples.
